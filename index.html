    So how should a child actually breathe?

    Nasal breathing

    The way it's meant to work

    • Warms, filters and moistens the air before it ever reaches the lungs
    • Lets the tongue rest against the roof of the mouth, which helps the jaw develop properly
    • Improves oxygen intake and supports deeper, more restful sleep
    • Triggers the release of nitric oxide, which helps open blood vessels and sharpen focus

    Mouth breathing

    A sign the body is compensating

    • The air arrives cold, dry and unfiltered, which irritates the tonsils and adenoids
    • Pushes the tongue down and out of position, so the upper jaw stays narrow and crowded
    • Leaves sleep lighter and more restless, with more waking through the night
    • Can shape how the face and jaw develop over time
